Rarefaction and alpha diversity analyses constitute essential components of microbiome research, offering complementary insights into the quality of sequencing data and the complexity of microbial communities within individual samples.
Rarefaction analysis evaluates whether the sequencing depth is sufficient to encompass the majority of microbial taxa present in a sample. By plotting the number of observed taxa against sequencing effort, rarefaction curves assist in determining if additional sequencing would likely uncover more diversity. Curves that approach a plateau signify adequate sampling, thereby increasing confidence that subsequent analyses are founded on a representative characterization of the microbial community. Additionally, rarefaction functions as a crucial quality-control measure by identifying samples with insufficient sequencing depth or uneven sampling effort.
Alpha diversity analysis measures the diversity within a single microbial community through the assessment of species richness and evenness. Common metrics such as Observed ASVs, Chao1, Shannon, and Simpson indices capture various facets of community structure. Richness-based metrics estimate the number of taxa present, whereas diversity indices consider both the number and relative abundances of taxa. These metrics offer valuable insights into ecological complexity, community stability, and microbial equilibrium.
In microbiome studies, variations in alpha diversity are frequently associated with physiological and pathological states. Reduced diversity has been consistently linked to dysbiosis and conditions such as inflammatory bowel disease, obesity, cardiovascular disease, and metabolic syndrome, whereas higher diversity typically indicates a more resilient and functionally robust microbial ecosystem. Comparing alpha diversity across different experimental groups enables researchers to assess the impacts of dietary interventions, therapeutic treatments, environmental factors, or disease progression on microbial community composition.
Collectively, rarefaction and alpha diversity analyses lay the groundwork for rigorous microbiome data interpretation. Rarefaction confirms the adequacy and comparability of sequencing effort across samples, while alpha diversity provides quantitative measures of within-sample microbial complexity. These analyses are indispensable prerequisites for reliable downstream investigations, including beta diversity, differential abundance testing, functional prediction, and ecological network analysis, thereby facilitating accurate biological interpretation and ensuring reproducibility in microbiome research.

This repository contains the R workflow used to perform rarefaction analysis and alpha diversity assessment of 16S rRNA gene amplicon sequencing data. The script imports an ASV abundance table, evaluates sequencing depth using rarefaction curves, computes within-sample diversity metrics, and generates publication-quality visualizations with accompanying statistical analyses.
The workflow calculates commonly used alpha diversity indices, including observed richness, Shannon diversity, and Simpson diversity, using the phyloseq and vegan R packages. Group-wise comparisons can be performed using non-parametric statistical tests, and the resulting figures are formatted for publication.
This pipeline is intended for reproducible microbiome diversity analyses and can be adapted to other amplicon sequencing datasets with minimal modification.
